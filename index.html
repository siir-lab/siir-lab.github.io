Our Mission

Transforming Workplace Safety Through Innovation

The Safety Innovation Integration Research (SIIR) Lab is committed to advancing the science and practice of occupational health and safety. We develop and deploy evidence-based interventions, digital tools, and data-driven frameworks that protect workers across construction, energy, and infrastructure sectors.

  • Integrating AI, wearable sensors, and computer vision to proactively detect and mitigate hazards.
  • Building inclusive safety frameworks that account for diverse worker populations and contexts.
  • Translating rigorous academic research into actionable industry practice.
  • Training the next generation of safety engineers and researchers through experiential learning.

Active Research

Projects

Our research spans AI-enabled safety monitoring, human factors, risk analytics, and technology integration in high-hazard work environments.

NSF Funded
AI & Computer Vision

Real-Time Hazard Detection Using Computer Vision on Construction Sites

This NSF-funded project develops deep-learning models that analyze live video feeds from construction sites to identify unsafe behaviors, PPE non-compliance, and proximity hazards in real time, enabling immediate corrective interventions.

NSF Funded
Wearable Technology

Wearable Biosensor Networks for Worker Fatigue and Heat-Stress Monitoring

Integrating physiological wearables with IoT infrastructure, this project tracks worker fatigue indicators and heat-stress markers in outdoor environments, feeding predictive analytics models to forecast and prevent safety incidents before they occur.

Learn More →
NSF Funded
Human Factors

Measuring and Improving Safety Climate in Diverse Construction Workforces

This project investigates how organizational culture, leadership behaviors, and workforce diversity influence safety climate perceptions, culminating in a validated multi-cultural assessment tool deployable across construction companies.

Learn More →
NSF Funded
Digital Twins

Digital Twin Framework for Proactive Construction Site Safety Management

By fusing BIM, IoT sensor streams, and worker positioning data, this project constructs a living digital replica of active job sites, enabling scenario simulation and risk assessment without interrupting ongoing work.

Learn More →
NSF Funded
Risk Analytics

Predictive Risk Modeling for Fall Prevention in High-Rise Construction

Falls remain the leading cause of fatalities in construction. This project leverages machine-learning risk models trained on historical incident data and site-condition variables to predict and rank fall-exposure hot spots, directing targeted interventions.

Learn More →
NSF Funded
XR Training

Immersive VR-Based Safety Training for Hazard Recognition and Response

This project develops and evaluates a suite of immersive virtual reality training modules that place workers inside photorealistic job-site hazard scenarios, measuring learning outcomes and knowledge retention against traditional classroom-based safety programs.

International Program

NSF IRES: LUMINY Research Exchange

The LUMINY International Research Experience for Students (IRES) program, supported by the National Science Foundation, sends SIIR Lab graduate and undergraduate students to collaborate with partner institutions in France, gaining exposure to European approaches to occupational safety, sustainable construction, and smart infrastructure.

Students spend 8–10 weeks embedded in host laboratories, co-authoring research, attending international conferences, and developing a global perspective on safety engineering challenges. This transformative experience cultivates the next cohort of globally competent safety researchers.

The Team

People

Lab Director & Principal Investigator

Dr. Chukwuma Nnaji

Assistant Professor · Zachry Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering
Texas A&M University

Dr. Nnaji leads the Safety Innovation Integration Research (SIIR) Laboratory at Texas A&M University. His research bridges occupational safety science, digital engineering, and human factors to produce scalable, evidence-based safety interventions for the construction and infrastructure sectors. He has authored over 20 peer-reviewed publications and secured more than $1 million in external research funding from agencies including the National Science Foundation.

Dr. Nnaji is a dedicated mentor who has supervised more than a dozen graduate students and undergraduate researchers. He is passionate about broadening participation in STEM and regularly engages with K–12 outreach initiatives and industry safety councils.
